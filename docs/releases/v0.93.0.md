# v0.93.0: Proof at scale

A test proves the route its author thought of. This release makes the engine search for the rest:
- whether a level can be won at all;
- how much room each jump leaves a person;
- what a careless player can break.

The agent doesn't have to play a thousand runs itself. It reads one answer, with the inputs that reproduce it.

```bash
npx @ludra/cli solve
npx @ludra/cli fuzz
```

## Saving and restoring a world

`World.save()` captures a moment and `World.restore(save)` brings it back, any number of times. The world then goes on bit for bit as it did the first time.
- **What it captures:** deep copies of the entities (references between them point at the copies), events, counters, the previous input, the seed, and the physics engine's own state.
- **Arcade** keeps everything on the entities.
- **Rapier 2D and 3D** save with Rapier's own snapshot and find their bodies, colliders and joints again by handle.
- **Proof:** a test hashes every frame of a run that was saved, sent on a different future and restored, against one that never stopped, on all three engines.
- **Cost:** on Moonlit Shrine a save costs about 1.5 simulation steps and a restore about 1.8. That is what makes searching fast: thousands of tries start from a saved moment instead of frame 0.

## `ludra solve`

Searches the inputs for a way to win. It remembers every distinct place the player reaches (with the score and every script's `state`, so a key taken counts as progress), and keeps exploring from the least-tried places nearest the goal.

```
$ ludra solve
✓ SOLVED: a win at 2.95s (frame 177)
  ludra play . --inputs "right:1.4s, right+jump:93f"

Timing
  jump    at 1.40s (frame 84)     window 6 frames (0.10s): 0 early, 5 late      tight

As a test (add it to a tests/*.test.json file's "tests" list)
  {"name":"the solver's route reaches the goal","inputs":"right:1.4s, right+jump:93f","assert":["won == true"]}

explored 122,381 frames, 410 cells, method save, 0.4s
```

- **The route** is simplified to read like a person's, proven by replaying it on a fresh world, and printed as a test to paste.
- **Timing windows:** each press is moved frame by frame until it no longer works. It is judged like a person would judge it: the same landing, the same pickups, no death. A press is labelled tight at 6 frames (0.1 s) or less, forgiving above 15.
- **When it can't win:** "every reachable place was tried" means unsolvable as far as the search can tell. "Ran out of time" means try `--time 2m`. Either way it prints the closest place a player stood, as a `ludra play … --screenshot end` line.
- **Options:** `--goal "<assertion>"` searches for any test assertion instead of a win (`--goal "score >= 5"`). There are also `--allow-deaths`, `--time`, `--budget`, `--seed` and `--json`.
- **Exit code:** 1 when unsolved.

Seven levels with answers measured by hand first ([`packages/cli/test/fixtures/solve`](https://github.com/ersingvn/ludra/tree/main/packages/cli/test/fixtures/solve)) check the solver:

| Level | Measured by hand | `ludra solve` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| easy-walk | fastest win frame 147 | `right:147f`, frame 147 |
| one-jump | jump window 20 frames | solved, window 20 frames |
| narrow-window | jump window 6 frames, from frame 84 | solved, window 6 frames at frame 84, tight |
| impossible | no win: the gap is 90 px past the longest jump | unsolved, exhausted in 0.6 s |
| softlock | jump window 28 frames | solved, window 28 frames |
| needs-key | key first; fastest win 3.58 s | solved by going left for the key first, win at 3.95 s |
| 3d-hop | three jumps on Rapier 3D; a hand sweep found a 4.37 s win | solved in 7 s, win at 5.27 s |

Every answer is right. On needs-key and 3d-hop the route isn't the fastest one: the search refines a win for a while, but doesn't prove it optimal.

## `ludra fuzz`

Plays hundreds of seeded random sessions, the way someone mashing keys would. It reports:
- **Crashes:** script errors, one per script, hook and cause, with the inputs that cause them.
- **Softlocks:** places a player can get into but never win from, and where nothing kills them. Each new place the player stands on is checked with a quick search from a save of that moment.
- **Where players die:** by cause, with the places it happens most (a difficulty map, not a bug list).

```
$ ludra fuzz
Played 200 random sessions: 711,902 frames in 2.1s, 397 distinct places, 4 lucky wins.

✗ SOFTLOCK at (386, 702): the player can get here (after 5.38s) but can never win from here, and doesn't die.
  ludra play . --inputs "jump:33f, left+jump:10f, jump:23f, left:46f, wait:14f, right:7f, left:28f, right:8f, wait:14f, jump:25f, right:8f, jump:0.3s, right+jump:53f, right:0.4s, wait:0.2s" --screenshot end
  fix: give the player a way out (a ladder, a spring, a ledge low enough to jump) or make falling there cost a life (a hazard or world.fallLimit).
```

- It first checks that the level can be won at all. If it can't, it says so (and exits 1) instead of calling every place a trap.
- Every finding comes with `ludra play --inputs` that reproduce it from frame 0.
- It exits 1 on crashes, softlocks or an unwinnable level. Deaths alone don't fail it.

## `ludra review` asks whether it's fair

A new **Fair** area:
- **`R_FRAME_PERFECT`** (issue): a press in the winning test's route that only works on one or two exact frames.
- **`R_TIGHT_TIMING`** (idea): a press that only works within 0.1 s. Fine for a hard moment, unfair early on.
- **`R_SOFTLOCK`** (issue) and **`R_CRASH`** (issue, under Proof): from a few seconds of random play per scene.
- `review --fast` skips the random play.

Every template and Moonlit Shrine review without issues under the new checks.

## Prove, in the skill's loop

The Claude Code skill's loop gains a step between Test and Review: run `solve` to prove the level can be beaten (and paste its route as a test), and `fuzz` to find crashes and softlocks. Fix every crash and softlock, and treat an unsolved level as a bug unless the user asked for one.

## Upgrading

- `npm install @ludra/cli@^0.93.0`. Games simulate exactly as before: the Rapier 2D and 3D golden hashes are unchanged, and every test from v0.92 passes untouched.
- Physics engines from outside Ludra keep working. Without `save`/`restore`, `solve` falls back to replaying inputs from frame 0, and `fuzz` skips the softlock checks.
